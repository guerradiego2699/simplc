## Why a PLC communicates

A PLC rarely works alone. It communicates to:

- Be **programmed** and checked from a computer.
- Show data on and take commands from an **HMI screen** at the machine.
- Send information to a **SCADA** system that supervises the whole plant.
- Talk to **other PLCs**, **variable frequency drives**, energy meters or robots.
- Read **remote I/O**: input and output modules installed far from the PLC, linked by a single
  communication cable instead of hundreds of signal wires.

It helps to separate two ideas: the **physical medium** (the cable and its electrical signals) and the
**protocol** (the language devices use to understand each other). The same RS-485 cable can carry Modbus
RTU or PROFIBUS, and the same Ethernet cable can carry Modbus TCP, PROFINET, EtherNet/IP or OPC UA.

## Physical media

### USB

Used to **program** and diagnose from a computer. It is convenient, but not meant for permanent
connections or long distances.

### RS-232

**Point-to-point** serial communication (one device with another). It reaches short distances, around
15 m. Today it appears mostly on older equipment, printers, barcode readers and scales.

### RS-485

Serial **bus** communication: several devices share the same twisted pair. It is very resistant to noise
because it uses differential signals (the voltage difference between wires A and B). It allows distances
of up to about **1200 m** at low speeds and has **termination resistors** at both ends of the bus. It is
the medium of Modbus RTU and PROFIBUS DP.

### Ethernet

The dominant medium today. It uses network cables with RJ45 connectors (or M12 connectors in the field),
**switches** and copper segments of up to **100 m**. For longer distances or very noisy environments,
fibre optics are used. Many PLCs have two ports with a built-in switch to daisy-chain devices.

## Industrial protocols

| Protocol             | Associated with                               | Physical medium            | Typical use                                    |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Modbus RTU           | Open (created by Modicon, now Schneider)      | RS-485 / RS-232            | Drives, meters, simple devices                 |
| Modbus TCP           | Open                                          | Ethernet                   | The same as Modbus RTU, over Ethernet          |
| PROFIBUS DP          | Siemens and the PI organization               | RS-485                     | Remote I/O and drives in existing plants       |
| PROFINET             | Siemens and the PI organization               | Ethernet                   | I/O, drives and real-time control              |
| EtherNet/IP          | Rockwell Automation and the ODVA organization | Ethernet                   | I/O and drives, very common with Allen-Bradley |
| CC-Link / CC-Link IE | Mitsubishi Electric and the CLPA organization | Dedicated cable / Ethernet | Machines and lines of Asian origin             |
| EtherCAT             | Beckhoff and the EtherCAT Technology Group    | Ethernet                   | Very fast motion control                       |
| OPC UA               | OPC Foundation (vendor independent)           | Ethernet (TCP/IP)          | Data exchange with SCADA, MES and IT systems   |

A few ideas to read the table:

- **Modbus** is simple, open and old (late 1970s). That is why almost every device speaks it, even as a
  second option. It works with a **master** (or client) that asks and **slaves** (or servers) that answer.
- **PROFINET**, **EtherNet/IP**, **CC-Link IE** and **EtherCAT** are **industrial** Ethernet: they add
  mechanisms to exchange data cyclically and predictably, which is key to controlling machines.
- **OPC UA** does not compete with them in fast control: it is used to carry data securely and in an
  organised way to higher-level systems, independently of the PLC brand.

<Callout type="industry">
  A real plant usually mixes protocols: PROFINET between the PLC and its drives, Modbus RTU with
  some old meters and OPC UA to the SCADA. When two networks do not understand each other, a
  **gateway** translates between them.
</Callout>

## HMI and SCADA

- An **HMI** (Human-Machine Interface) is the screen, usually a touch screen, that the operator has next
  to the machine. It shows states, alarms and values, and accepts commands such as starting a cycle or
  changing a recipe.
- A **SCADA** (Supervisory Control And Data Acquisition) system **supervises** a whole plant or
  installations spread over a region, such as a drinking-water network. It gathers data from many PLCs,
  keeps history, manages alarms and shows everything in control rooms.

In both cases, **the control logic stays in the PLC**. The HMI and the SCADA read and write PLC variables,
but if communication is lost, the PLC must keep controlling the process safely.

<Callout type="warning">
  A command coming from an HMI or a SCADA must never bypass the program's safety conditions: the PLC
  has to validate every command before moving an actuator.
</Callout>

## Industrial network security

Plant networks (OT networks, for Operational Technology) control physical equipment, so an attack or a
mistake can stop production or damage machines. Some basic rules:

- **Never expose a PLC directly to the internet.**
- Separate the control network from the office network with security devices such as firewalls.
- For remote access, use solutions designed for it (for example, a VPN) and controlled by the plant.
- Change default passwords and disable services that are not used.

The **IEC 62443** series is the international reference for cybersecurity in industrial automation and
control systems.

## Summary

- Tell the **physical medium** (USB, RS-232, RS-485, Ethernet) from the **protocol** (Modbus, PROFINET…).
- **RS-485** is a robust, long-range serial bus; **Ethernet** is the dominant medium today.
- Each major brand pushes an industrial Ethernet; **Modbus** and **OPC UA** are open.
- The **HMI** is the machine's screen; **SCADA** supervises the plant. The logic stays in the PLC.
- Control networks are protected: no PLC exposed to the internet.
